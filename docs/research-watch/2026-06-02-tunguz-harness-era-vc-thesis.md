# Research Watch: "Software After AI: The Harness Era Begins" — Tom Tunguz

- Repo/Link: https://tomtunguz.com/harnessing-ai/
- Source: GeekNews

## Why this is worth watching
Tom Tunguz is a managing director at Theory Ventures; his framing essays demonstrably shift startup formation patterns. This piece names "the harness" as the post-SaaS competitive moat and proposes a 7-component model — when a VC of this reach publishes a named taxonomy, startups begin self-describing against it within months. The essay is a leading indicator of a tooling wave, not a lagging one.

## What stands out immediately
- 7-component harness model: context/memory, tools/action, orchestration/loop, state/persistence, sandbox/compute, observability/governance, cost/workflow optimization
- Framing harness quality — not model access — as the durable moat: "What happens when every company has access to the same model? The best riders win."
- Explicit claim: "The end of the software era is the beginning of the harness era."
- No specific tools cited; this is pure conceptual framing, not a product announcement
- The 7-component breakdown maps with high fidelity to clawfit's L2–L5 layers (harness → governance/SSOT → capabilities → memory/context)

## Why clawfit should care
The Tunguz taxonomy is a market-naming event. Once VCs fund startups against a named map, the registry will receive submissions from tools that self-classify using this vocabulary. clawfit's 7-layer model already covers the same terrain; the risk is vocabulary drift — tools describing themselves as "harness components" that span multiple clawfit layers. The scoring model has no current mechanism to handle tools that explicitly bundle, say, orchestration + observability as a single product claim.

## Preliminary interpretation
Current best reading:
- **Level 2 — Meta wrappers / harness / orchestration layer** (conceptual anchor for L2 naming; components 6 and 3 also touch L3 governance and L4 capabilities respectively)
- This is a thought-leadership signal, not a tool registration candidate — no code, no repo
